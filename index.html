Penguin Species 🐧

There are 18 different penguin species in the world

👑

Emperor Penguin

Aptenodytes forsteri

The largest penguin species. It grows up to 1.2 metres and can reach 45 kg. It lives in the coldest conditions of Antarctica and withstands -60°C!

🧊 Antarctica 👑 Largest ❤️ Loyal Partner
🤴

King Penguin

Aptenodytes patagonicus

The second largest species after the emperor penguin. Known for its bright orange-gold neck feathers. Its chicks are adorable in their fluffy brown coats!

🏝️ Sub-Antarctic 🧡 Orange Neck 📏 95cm
🎩

Adélie Penguin

Pygoscelis adeliae

One of the best known species, with its classic "tuxedo" look. Curious and bold by nature. They give pebbles as gifts to the ones they love!

💎 Pebble Gift 🧊 Antarctica 🏃 Fast
🎸

Macaroni Penguin

Eudyptes chrysolophus

Looking like a rock star with its yellow-orange crest, this species forms the most crowded penguin colonies. A single colony can hold more than 100,000 pairs!

🎵 Rock Star 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Crowded 🌊 Ocean
💙

Little Blue Penguin

Eudyptula minor

The smallest penguin species in the world! Just 33 cm tall and 1 kg in weight, this tiny penguin lives along the coasts of Australia and New Zealand.

🔬 Smallest 🦘 Australia 🌙 Nocturnal
🌴

Galápagos Penguin

Spheniscus mendiculus

The only penguin species living north of the equator! It survives on tropical islands thanks to the cold Humboldt current. One of the rare endangered species.

🌺 Tropical 🌊 Humboldt ⚠️ Endangered

Fun Facts 🎉

Surprising things you did not know about penguins

💍

Proposing With a Pebble

Adélie penguins propose by offering the finest pebble they can find to the female they like. If she approves of the pebble, they build a nest together!

🫂

Huddling for Warmth

To survive -60°C cold, emperor penguins form a huge group called a "huddle". They take turns moving to the centre to warm up!

🏃‍♂️

Sliding on Their Bellies

Instead of walking, penguins can lie on their bellies and slide. This move is called "tobogganing" and it saves a lot of energy!

🧂

They Can Drink Salt Water

Penguins have a special gland behind their eyes. It filters the excess salt out of sea water, so they can drink straight from the ocean!

🎵

A Unique Call

Every penguin has its own distinct call. Among thousands of birds they can find their partner and their chicks by that unique sound alone!

🦕

60 Million Years of History

Penguins have lived on Earth for 60 million years! Their ancestors shared the planet with dinosaurs and were birds that could fly!
